News from the lab

Graduations, new papers, new people and everything else happening in QMAP.

Nov 2026

Congratulations, Dr. Muhammad Hassan Shaikh!

Hassan graduated with his PhD in Physics. His thesis explored magneto-optics of van der Waals antiferromagnet and TMD heterostructures, including the group's 2025 ACS Nano paper on reading out magnetism with 2D defects. Congratulations, Hassan!

Oct 2026

New preprint: polarization control of hBN spin-defect ensembles

PhD candidate Aqiq Ishraq's work on symmetry engineering in hexagonal boron nitride is now on arXiv. Nano-ridge strain lines up the emission polarization of an entire boron-vacancy ensemble. Read it on arXiv.

Fall 2026

Welcome, Reynold Gao!

Reynold (Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering) joined the group to study interfacial magnetism in antiferromagnet and TMD van der Waals heterostructures.

2026

New papers in Advanced Materials and Small Methods

Hassan Shaikh led two papers out this year. In Advanced Materials, light is used to switch chiral magnetic states in the van der Waals antiferromagnet CrPS4. In Small Methods, a lens-enhanced optical transfer technique makes van der Waals stacking both precise and scalable.

Jun 2026

Prof. Chakraborty receives an NSF CAREER Award

The five-year, $500,000 award from the National Science Foundation will develop sensors that use light-emitting defects in ultrathin semiconductors to detect magnetic fields only a few angstroms from a surface, with possible uses from quantum technology to wearable brain-imaging devices. It also funds student training and a new job-shadowing program with industry partners. Read the UDaily story.

Apr 2026

Hassan Shaikh wins an MRS Graduate Student Silver Award

Hassan received a Graduate Student Silver Award at the 2026 MRS Spring Meeting, which recognizes particularly significant and timely research presented by a student.

Jan 2026

Bringing hidden magnetic states to light

UDaily featured the group's ACS Nano paper, led by Hassan Shaikh with Aqiq Ishraq and Collin Maurtua, showing that defects in a 2D semiconductor can optically read out the magnetic state of an antiferromagnet. Read the UDaily story.
